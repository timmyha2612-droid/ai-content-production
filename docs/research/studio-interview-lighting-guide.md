# Studio interview lighting

A working guide for lighting sit-down interviews in the studio. It is built from a private Cosmos board, [Studio interview lighting](https://www.cosmos.so/timmy111/studio-interview-lighting), which has 9 sub-boards: 8 lighting setups and 1 board of behind-the-scenes (BTS) rigs.

It goes with the shotcraft lighting guide (`docs/research/cinematic-lighting-guide.md`). That guide covers lighting in general. This one covers studio interviews only: what to rig, where the cameras go, and what usually goes wrong.

Reference frames link to `https://www.cosmos.so/e/<elementId>`. Most numbers in brackets in the notes are element IDs on the board.

---

## How to pick a setup

Choose on three things.

**1. Client and subject.**
- Serious personal stories (grief, comebacks, crime, legacy) need a dark, intimate look: **Documentary soft key**, **Low-key black void** or **Window-style side light**.
- Corporate, charity and testimonial series need to look clean, trustworthy and consistent: **Classic three-point** or **High-key white**.
- Founders, authors and wellness or lifestyle brands want warmth and trust: **Dressed set with practicals** or **Window-style**.
- Tech, music, youth and creator brands want energy and brand colour: **Coloured background wash**.
- Two or more people at a table: **Podcast / multi-person**.

**2. Tone.**
- Intimate and serious: dark background and little or no fill.
- Open and honest: bright, low contrast.
- Warm and premium: practicals in a dressed set.
- Bold and branded: colour on the wall, never on the face.

**3. Platform.**
- Long-form YouTube or podcast: a set you can light once and run for hours. Use one fixed key per seat.
- Social vertical (9:16, 4:5): a clean or bold background holds captions well. High-key and coloured wash work best.
- Broadcast or streaming documentary: soft key on a dark background, low-key void, or window style.
- Corporate intranet or website: three-point or high-key. Above all, it must match across a day of different people.

**The rule under every setup:** one big soft key, close to the face, on the side the subject is looking towards. Shadow control (negative fill) does the shaping. The background is lit, or kept dark, separately from the face.

## 1. Documentary interview: soft key, dark background

Sub-board: https://www.cosmos.so/timmy111/documentary-interview-soft-key-dark-background

**Look.** One big, soft key on the short side of the face (the side turned away from camera). There is little or no fill, so the shadow side falls into a smooth dark. The background sits 2-4 stops under the face, broken up by a few soft accents: a warm lamp, a slash of light on the wall or floor, sometimes a low cool LED strip. It feels intimate, serious and cinematic, the "Netflix doc" mood. Skin keeps its texture and both eyes keep a big round catchlight.

**Best for.**
- Documentary sit-downs (sport, music, founder and personal stories)
- Brand films and testimonials that should feel premium and honest
- Long-form podcast sets (a two-shot at a table)
- Grief, comeback, mental health and legacy stories
- Executive, author and artist profiles
- Black-and-white portrait interviews

**Rig, light by light.**
- **Key:** a 120-150 cm deep octa or parabolic, or a 4x4 to 6x6 diffusion frame. Put it on the short side, 30-60° off the eyeline and 30-45° above eye height, angled down. Keep it about 1-1.5 m from the face and feather it so the hot centre sits just in front of the face. At a podcast table, one big overhead lantern or octa pushed slightly towards the guest also works.
- **Negative fill:** a 4x4 black floppy (a black panel that soaks up bounce) 1-1.5 m off the shadow cheek. Usually there is no fill light at all. If you need some, use a far-off white bounce or a very low LED at least 2-3 stops under the key.
- **Flags on the key:** a skirt or grid on the octa, plus top and floor flags, so the room can drop to black, even in a white studio.
- **Background accent 1 (practical):** a warm tungsten lamp, Edison bulb or salt lamp in the back third of frame. Dim it so the shade glows without clipping.
- **Background accent 2 (slash or pool):** a fresnel, an LED with barn doors, or a gobo (a cut-out pattern) raking across the back wall or floor. Keep it 1-2 stops under the face.
- **Cool accent (optional):** an LED strip or tube on the floor at the back wall, blue or teal, very low.
- **Rim (optional):** a small LED behind on the key side, only to pull dark hair or clothes off black. Often left out.
- **Two-handers:** each person gets their own short-side soft key, or one big overhead source sits between them. Keep the space between them dark.

**Background.** Keep the subject 3-5 m or more from the wall. Expose the background 2-4 stops under the face: dark grey to near-black, with no big areas of clipped black. Use textured or dark surfaces (brick, concrete, teal-grey paint, slats). Leave a floor plane or corner visible so it reads as a room, not a cut-out.

**Camera.** A-cam: 35-50 mm (full-frame equivalent) MCU (medium close-up, chest up). B-cam: 85-100 mm tight CU (close-up). Add a 24-35 mm locked-off wide for podcasts. Shoot at T2-T2.8. The interviewer sits beside the A lens on the shadow side, so the subject looks into the dark side of frame. Put B on the same side of the eyeline as A, 20-30° further round. Frame the eyes on the upper third with space in the direction they look.

**Colour.** Key at 4300-5000 K with the camera at 4800-5600 K, so skin sits natural to slightly warm. Practicals at 2700-3000 K. Cool accents at 6500-8000 K, very low. In the grade, lift the blacks slightly, desaturate the shadows and roll off the highlights gently. Black and white is a valid finish.

**Small-kit version (3 lights).**
1. Key: a 300-600 W COB (Aputure 300d/600d class) in a 120 cm deep octa with a grid. Short side, about 45°, slightly above eye level, 1.2 m away.
2. Negative fill: a black 4x4 or a V-flat (two boards hinged into a V) on the shadow side, plus a flag between the key and the wall.
3. Background: a 2700 K dimmable table lamp, and/or a small barn-doored LED raking a slash across the wall.

Optional: a low blue tube on the floor, or a faint rim. The minimum is two lights: the soft key plus one practical, with a black floppy.

**Common mistakes.**
- Key on the broad side (the camera side of the face). The face goes flat.
- Adding fill "to be safe". It kills the falloff.
- Key spill turning the back wall grey. Grid it, flag it, and move the subject forward.
- A pure black void with no accents. It looks cheap.
- Practicals clipping, or the lamp becoming the brightest thing in frame.
- Key too small or too far away (a bare panel at 3 m).
- Key too high (raccoon eyes, no catchlight) or too frontal.
- Mixed colour by accident: green-spiking LEDs or a window leaking in.
- Strong saturated RGB accents ("podcast purple").
- B-cam across the eyeline, so it sees the broad side.
- Shooting at f/8, so the background is sharp and busy.

**Prompt phrases.**
- documentary interview still, seated subject lit by a large soft key light from the side
- short-side lighting, soft wraparound key, deep shadow falloff, no fill
- dark moody studio background, underexposed room, subtle texture on the back wall
- warm tungsten practical table lamp glowing softly in the background
- soft diagonal slash of light on the back wall, pool of light on the floor
- subtle cool blue LED accent low on the back wall
- cinematic 50mm medium close-up, shallow depth of field, creamy bokeh
- natural skin texture, large round catchlight in the eyes
- Netflix documentary interview look, filmic contrast, gently lifted blacks, desaturated shadows

- https://www.cosmos.so/e/1011758216: the result from that rig. Short-side wrap, no fill, warm brick, cool floor accent.

## 2. Classic three-point interview

Sub-board: https://www.cosmos.so/timmy111/classic-three-point-interview

**Look.** Clean, modelled and trustworthy. A large soft key lights the side of the face away from the lens, so the far cheek sits 1-1.5 stops darker. A rim or hair light separates the subject from a background that sits darker than the face. It feels "professional but human": nothing harsh and nothing flat.

**Best for.**
- Corporate and brand testimonials
- Documentary sit-downs
- Church, non-profit and charity films
- One-on-one podcasts and talk shows
- Executive and founder interviews
- Testimonial series that must match across a day
- Talking-head explainers and course content

**Rig, light by light.**
- **Key:** a 90-120 cm gridded deep octa or parabolic, or a 4x4 to 6x6 frame of grid cloth. Place it 30-50° off the lens on the side the subject looks towards (short side). Put the centre 20-40 cm above the eyes, angled down, so the catchlights sit at 10 or 2 o'clock. Keep it as close as the frame allows.
- **Key control:** a grid, plus a floppy on the lens side of the key to stop spill and flare.
- **Fill:** often none. Use a black floppy 1-1.5 m off the shadow cheek. For a brighter corporate look, add a bounce near the lens at 1/4 to 1/8 of the key, at eye height or a bit below.
- **Back / rim (kicker):** a small Fresnel or 60-120 W COB 120-160° from camera, opposite the key, 2.2-3 m high, aimed down at the hair and shoulder. Flag it off the lens.
- **Hair / top (optional):** an overhead boom light for dark hair on a dark background.
- **Background:** a separate small unit with a snoot or grid putting a soft pool behind the head. Or use tubes and practicals hidden in the set, kept 1-2 stops under the face, or an even wash on a coloured seamless for a series.
- **Haze (optional):** a light haze so the back light and practicals bloom.

**Background.** Keep the subject at least 1.5-3 m off the backdrop. There are two schools:
1. A coloured seamless (pale blue, sage, teal) lit on its own, about 0.5-1.5 stops under the face. Light it evenly for a series, or with a gradient pool for mood.
2. A real office, loft or living room. Pull the ambient down, flag the key off the walls, and keep practicals and windows as accents.

Always keep a darker zone directly behind the head and a brighter accent off to one side.

**Camera.** A-cam: 50-85 mm at T2-T2.8, just off the eyeline. The interviewer sits beside the lens and the subject looks slightly off-axis. B-cam: 85-135 mm tighter CU, 20-40° away in angle, often on the opposite side for a profile cut. Framing ladder: wide, then medium, then a tight CU that cuts the forehead. Leave look-room. Podcasts use 2-3 cameras.

**Colour.** Key at 5600 K, or 4300-5000 K to match a mixed location. Practicals at 2700-3200 K. The rim matches the key or sits slightly warmer. A cool backdrop against warm skin works well. Grade with gentle contrast, lifted (not milky) blacks and a slightly desaturated background.

**Small-kit version (3 lights).**
1. Key: a 300-600 W COB in a 90 cm gridded parabolic, 40° on the side the subject faces (short side), 1-1.2 m away, 30 cm above the eyes, with the lens side flagged.
2. Rim: a 60-150 W COB or small Fresnel, high, 130-150° behind on the opposite side, at 1/2 to equal of the key's reading on the hair.
3. Background: a small gridded or snooted LED pool on the wall 2 m behind, or a practical.

Use a white bounce or a black floppy instead of a fill light. With two lights, drop the background light and add a practical. With one light plus a window, use the window as the rim.

**Common mistakes.**
- Key too small or far away.
- Broad lighting: the key on the side the subject faces camera.
- A second softbox as fill, giving TV-news flatness and double nose shadows.
- Subject against the backdrop: no separation, and the rim cannot reach.
- Rim flaring the lens, or too hot on bald heads.
- Background brighter than the face, or practicals blown out.
- No catchlights, or doubled ones.
- Glare in glasses.
- Key fighting window daylight with no correction.
- B-cam on the same angle as A, so the cuts jump.

**Prompt phrases.**
- seated documentary interview, medium close-up, subject looking just off camera
- large soft key light from the side, short-side lighting, gentle shadow falloff on far cheek
- subtle hair and rim light separating subject from background
- softly lit seamless backdrop with gentle vignette glow behind the head
- cinematic corporate testimonial, 85mm lens, shallow depth of field
- natural skin tones, soft catchlights, low contrast fill

**Reference frames.**

- https://www.cosmos.so/e/1350299541: Modern Wisdom podcast. Huge overhead silk, hard back source.
- https://www.cosmos.so/e/2016793650: CU, MCU and BTS of one location interview. A framing ladder with off-axis A/B.
- https://www.cosmos.so/e/1001429057: wood office. Glasses handled, profile B-cam.

## 3. High-key white studio interview

Sub-board: https://www.cosmos.so/timmy111/high-key-white-studio-interview

**Look.** Bright, airy and low-contrast. Subjects sit in a white or light-grey seamless space with no visible horizon. Soft top-front light gives gentle shadows under the brow and chin. The white floor bounces natural fill back up. The background sits 1-2 stops over the face, or rolls off to light grey with a soft hot spot between the chairs (the Variety look). It feels open, honest, modern and editorial. Wardrobe and chairs supply the colour.

**Best for.**
- Two-person conversation formats (Actors on Actors, brand talk shows)
- Corporate testimonials and leadership interviews
- Personal-branding and lifestyle talking heads
- Fashion, beauty, design and architecture content
- Podcasts on a seamless set
- Social vertical cuts that need room for captions
- Clients who want "Apple / editorial clean"

**Rig, light by light.**
- **Key, one per subject:** a 120-150 cm parabolic or deep octa (for example Godox QR-P120, Aputure Light Dome 150), or a 4x4 to 6x6 frame, on a boom. Place it 30-45° off the nose towards the eyeline, 1-1.5 m above head height, tilted down 30-45°, and feathered in front of the face.
- **Overhead ambient / top fill:** a large dome, space light, china ball or balloon light (80-120 cm), centred high (3-4 m) to fill the eye sockets evenly.
- **Wrap / side softs:** gridded strip banks or tall softboxes at about 90° on each side, at eye-to-head height, to wrap the cheeks and separate dark clothes.
- **Large-source alternative:** a fresnel pushed through an 8x8 or 6x6 frame of 1/2 grid or 250 diffusion at 45°. It gives a soft but directional, window-like key.
- **Cyc lights:** rows of soft units or cyc lights aimed at the cyc, never at the talent. Set them 1-2 stops over the face for pure white, or about level for light grey. A lightly flagged spot between the chairs makes the halo.
- **Flags / negative fill:** black floppies above and beside each key to stop cyc light spilling onto faces. Add a black solid on the far side of the face when you want shape. Without negative fill a white room flattens every face.
- **Bounce:** the white floor is the fill. A grey rug under the chairs cuts it back.
- **Backlight:** rarely needed. Use a small gridded top-back light only when hair blends into the cyc.

**Background.** Use a white or light-grey cyc or paper, with the subject 3-5 m in front. For pure white, light it evenly until it is 1-2 stops over the face and just clips at the top of the curve. For editorial grey, under-light it so it falls to mid-grey at the top, with a soft pool behind and between the subjects. On location, a 2.7 m white paper roll plus one big overhead octa does the job. Keep props minimal: chairs, a side table, a plant, a rug, and booms shown on purpose.

**Camera.** At least three cameras. A: a 24-35 mm locked-off, centred wide showing the cyc (in editorial versions the booms and stands can show). B and C: 50-85 mm singles from just beside the other person's shoulder, 15-30° off the eyeline. Use f/2.8-f/4, because the blank background has nothing to blur. Expose for skin and let the cyc clip gently. Vertical crops work well.

**Colour.** Use 5600 K everywhere, with the cyc and the key matched so the white does not go cyan or magenta. You can warm the whole look to 4300-5000 K for a creamy white. No gels. Watch for green spikes from mixed cyc LEDs. Grade with low contrast, slightly lifted blacks and natural, slightly warm skin. B&W works very well.

**Small-kit version (2-3 lights).**
1. Key: a 120 cm parabolic on a boom, about 45° to the side the subject faces, 1 m above the eyes.
2. Background: a COB or 60 cm softbox behind the subject aimed at the paper, flagged off the subject, pushing the paper 1-1.5 stops over the face. For two people, aim it at the centre of the paper for the halo.
3. Optional: a china ball as top fill, or a white 4x4 bounce.

Keep the subject at least 2-3 m from the paper. Use a grey rug if the faces go flat.

**Common mistakes.**
- Lighting the cyc and the face with the same units, so the background goes muddy and the face goes flat.
- Subject too close to the backdrop: wrap-around spill and grey shadows on the paper.
- No negative fill, giving a passport-photo face.
- Cyc over-exposed, blooming into the subject's edges and lowering contrast.
- Keys straight overhead, giving dark eye sockets.
- White wardrobe, so the subject disappears.
- Mismatched colour temperature between the cyc and the key.
- Stands and flags in the wide by accident.
- Too much floor bounce on a shiny face. Use powder and a grey rug.

**Prompt phrases.**
- high-key white studio interview
- two people seated facing each other on an infinite white cyclorama
- soft overhead parabolic softbox lighting, gentle wraparound fill
- bright evenly lit white seamless background, low contrast, soft shadows
- boom microphones visible above, editorial conversation series
- wide locked-off two-shot, 35mm lens, centred symmetrical composition
- airy, modern, honest, magazine editorial video still

- https://www.cosmos.so/e/898887360: BTS. Parabolic key, three lanterns as top fill, overhead flags.
- https://www.cosmos.so/e/1956335272: the purest version, with rows of units and black flags on show.

## 4. Low-key black void interview

Sub-board: https://www.cosmos.so/timmy111/low-key-black-void-interview

**Look.** The subject floats in black. One soft-to-medium source carves the face from the side or above. The shadow side goes to near-black and nothing behind the subject reads, apart from an occasional pool of light on the floor. Contrast runs from about 4:1 to 8:1 or more. It feels confessional, serious and premium, like Errol Morris crossed with Variety. Wides read as theatre: one chair, one light, an empty dark room.

**Best for.**
- Testimony, grief, survival and true-crime interviews
- Founder or CEO origin stories that need gravitas
- Celebrity and artist sit-downs
- Brand manifestos and cinematic testimonial ads
- Single-guest specials where the set should vanish
- Dramatic wide establishers of a lone chair

**Rig, light by light.**
- **Key, soft version:** a 4-5 ft octa or a 4x4 frame of grid cloth, 45-70° off the lens on the short side, 20-40° above the eyeline, about 1-1.5 m from the face. You get a soft catchlight and a gentle Rembrandt patch (a small triangle of light on the shadow cheek).
- **Key, hard/top version for wides:** one fresnel, open-face or reflector LED, high at three-quarter back/side, 2-3 m away, throwing a pool on the floor. Or a big lantern hung straight overhead.
- **Grid and skirt:** a 40° grid plus a black skirt or floppy on the background side. This is what keeps the void black.
- **Negative fill:** a black 4x4 close on the shadow side. In a white-walled studio this matters as much as the key does.
- **Fill (optional):** none, or a bounce or very low LED near the lens 2-3 stops under, just enough to see the far eye. For deep skin tones, add a little specular (shiny) key rather than more fill.
- **Edge / hair (optional):** a faint rim from a 1x1 LED or barn-doored fresnel behind. Leave it out for the purest void.
- **Floor pool (wides):** let the key make a soft oval around the chair, flagged off the walls.

**Background.** Use black duvetyne or velvet, or a black studio, with the subject 3-4 m from the wall when possible. The wall sits 4-6 stops under the face. A black floor, or a pool that fades to black at the edges. A faint texture or dark-grey falloff is fine if it comes from key spill, not from a background light. Warn talent that black clothes will vanish.

**Camera.** A-cam: 50-85 mm MCU. Tight CU: 85-135 mm. Use T2.8-T4, because the darkness separates the subject, not shallow focus. The interviewer sits tight beside the lens on the far side from the key. An on-axis Interrotron (a device that lets the subject look straight into the lens while seeing the interviewer) suits confessional moments. B-cam: a 30-45° side angle on the key side for a profile CU, or on the shadow side for something darker. C-cam: a 24-35 mm locked-off wide of the chair in its pool.

**Colour.** Key at 4300-5000 K, balanced at 5000-5600 K. Grade cool and desaturated in the shadows, or warm amber for an editorial feel. Teal shadows against warm skin suit darker skin tones. Keep the void neutral black and never gel it. B&W is a valid deliverable. Any rim matches the key or sits slightly cooler.

**Small-kit version (2 lights plus flags).**
1. Key: a 300-600 W COB in a 90-120 cm gridded parabolic, 60° off-axis on the short side, about 30° up, 1-1.2 m away.
2. Edge (optional): a 60-100 W LED, high three-quarter back, at low output.

Optional third: a tiny bounce 3 stops under as documentary-safe fill. Hang black duvetyne, or pull the subject 2-3 m from the darkest wall. Kill all room lights. Expose the bright side of the face around 55-65 IRE on lighter skin and keep the background under about 5 IRE (IRE is the 0-100 brightness scale on a waveform). In a small white room, negative fill and a grid are essential.

**Common mistakes.**
- An ungridded softbox, so the void goes muddy grey.
- Subject too close to the backdrop.
- Too much fill, or white walls bouncing, so the face goes flat.
- Broad lighting.
- Key too low or too frontal.
- Key too high, with no catchlight.
- Dark hair and black clothes with no edge, so the head merges with the background.
- Deep skin underexposed or lit too softly.
- Crushing blacks in camera on 8-bit codecs, causing banding. Crush in the grade instead.
- Stands or the interviewer reflected in glasses, or visible in the B-cam.

**Prompt phrases.**
- low-key interview portrait, subject in a black void
- single soft key light from the side, deep shadow falloff to black
- chiaroscuro lighting, Rembrandt patch on the cheek
- pool of light on the floor around a lone chair, long shadow
- subtle hair rim light separating the subject from the black background
- cinematic Errol Morris style sit-down, intimate and serious
- black and white high-contrast portrait, crisp profile edge light

- https://www.cosmos.so/e/679395652: overhead silk plus bounce, warm tungsten, sculpted cheekbones.
- https://www.cosmos.so/e/262359003: woven overhead lantern hung low, with fast falloff.
- https://www.cosmos.so/e/1796379645: softbox wrapped in black flags close to a dark backdrop.

## 5. Dressed set with practicals (lamps, shelves, bokeh)

Sub-board: https://www.cosmos.so/timmy111/dressed-set-with-practicals-lamps-shelves-bokeh

**Look.** A warm, lived-in room: a living room, library or members' club. One big, soft, hard-to-place key lights the face. Behind sits a dressed set in mid-tones and shadow, with dimmed warm practicals (lamps, Edison bulbs, sconces, shelf LEDs) going soft in the bokeh (the out-of-focus blur). The practicals make the audience believe the room lights the person, even though it does not. Contrast is about 3:1 to 6:1. The background sits 1.5-3 stops under the face but is never black. There are two flavours: the moody single-lamp table, and the brighter warm studio with lit shelves.

**Best for.**
- Long-form podcasts and video podcasts
- Founder, author and thought-leader interviews
- Documentary sit-downs in a "home" or "study"
- Recurring brand sets (YouTube channels, company podcasts)
- Wellness, finance and lifestyle clients
- Testimonials where a plain backdrop feels cold

**Rig, light by light.**
- **Key:** a 120-150 cm parabolic or octa, or a 1.2-2 m round diffusion. Hang it 30-50 cm above the eyeline, tilted down 30-45°, 20-45° off the lens on the side the subject faces, 1-1.5 m away. At a table, one disc over the centre works as a soft toplight for both people. Grid it when the walls are close.
- **Practicals:** 1-3 warm sources at different depths, dimmed to 25-60% (or 15-25 W equivalent bulbs). Each sits in the negative space beside the head, never growing out of it.
- **Set wash:** separate from the key. LED tape under shelves, small spots grazing the wall to make pools, and one accent colour (magenta, teal or cyan) on a plant or corner. Keep it 1-2 stops under the face.
- **Edge / kick (optional):** a small gridded strip 120-150° behind, at low level. The practicals often do this job already.
- **Fill:** usually none. The tabletop and walls bounce enough. Use negative fill on the shadow side if a light wall flattens the face. Add a top flag or skirt to keep the key off the floor.
- **Control:** a skirt or grid on every soft source, black wrap on bare bulbs that flare, and a dimmer on everything. Expose for the face, then bring each practical up until it just reads.

**Background.** A real dressed set 2-4 m behind: wood, books, curtains, plants, a rug, plaster, in warm browns, deep greens, teal or navy. Light it in pools with dark gaps, never flat. Keep the face the brightest thing in frame. Use T1.8-T2.8 on the singles so bulbs turn into round bokeh. Place practicals at several depths so the frame reads in layers.

**Camera.** A-cam: a 50-85 mm MCU, slightly off-axis, subject slightly off-centre with a practical in the empty side. B-cam: a 24-35 mm wide of the set. An over-the-shoulder (OTS) on 35-50 mm with the interviewer soft in the foreground adds depth. Close down a little on the wide so the set reads. Camera at eye level or just below. A 1/8 Black Pro-Mist (a diffusion filter that softens highlights) suits the look. Keep the overhead key out of the top of the wide, unless showing it is a deliberate choice.

**Colour.** Key at 4300-5000 K. Practicals and shelf LEDs at 2400-3000 K. One cool or magenta accent. The key must sit a few hundred kelvin cooler than the lamps, or the skin goes orange. Grade to warm mids with slightly lifted warm blacks and neutral skin.

**Small-kit version (3 lights).**
1. Key: a 200-300 W COB in a 90-120 cm gridded parabolic, high, about 30° off-axis, 1-1.2 m away.
2. Set: a roll of dimmable warm LED tape, or two small RGB tubes hidden behind shelves or a plant.
3. Practicals: a shaded table lamp and a paper or Edison lamp with dimmable 2700 K bulbs at different depths.

Use a black floppy for negative fill. Keep talent at least 2 m from the wall and shoot at T2-2.8.

**Common mistakes.**
- Practicals so bright they clip.
- Expecting the practical to light the face.
- A lamp growing out of someone's head.
- Key too big, too frontal, or with too much fill, so the mood goes flat.
- Key spill making the set as bright as the face.
- Everything at 2700 K, so the skin goes orange.
- The key's edge in the top of the wide.
- Shelves dressed but unlit, so they read as clutter.
- Too many accent colours (it starts to look like a gaming room).
- Talent too close to the set: no bokeh, no depth.

**Prompt phrases.**
- seated interview in a warmly dressed studio set, cinematic documentary still
- shaded table lamp glowing warm in the background, practical lamps at different depths
- bookshelves with warm under-shelf lighting, pools of amber light on wood panelling
- shallow depth of field, creamy bokeh from Edison bulbs and ceiling spots
- warm-cool contrast, amber practicals against a teal-shadowed wall
- intimate podcast conversation at a wooden table, overhead soft light, lamp beside guest

## 6. Coloured background wash and gels

Sub-board: https://www.cosmos.so/timmy111/coloured-background-wash-and-gels

**Look.** A natural-skinned subject in front of a strong colour field: teal, magenta, deep blue, amber, or a two-colour split. The face reads as normal daylight. All the attitude sits behind the subject as a pool, gradient or uplight on the wall, sometimes with a coloured rim. It feels modern, branded and energetic. Dark versions feel moody and premium. Bright versions feel pop. The key point is that the colour stays off the skin.

**Best for.**
- Branded long-form podcasts and two-person sets
- Tech, start-up, music and youth-brand interviews
- Creator and YouTube talking heads, course content
- Recruitment and employer-brand pieces
- Launch testimonials where the brand colour must show
- Social vertical cutdowns that need to stop the scroll

**Rig, light by light.**
- **Key:** a 90-150 cm octa, parabolic or gridded 4x4, ungelled, at 5600 K (or 4300-5000 K). Place it 30-45° off the lens on the short side, 20-40° up, about 1-1.5 m away. For two people, give each their own, or use one big octa high and front-centre.
- **Grid / flag on the key:** essential, so white spill does not wash out the colour. V-flats on both sides of the set help.
- **Fill:** a low bounce or reflector from the camera side, or none plus negative fill for a moodier look. Aim for 2:1 to 4:1.
- **Background colour light:** one or two units aimed only at the wall, 2-4 m behind the subject. Option A: a hard spot with a projection lens or snoot and a gel, throwing a circle behind the head. Option B: RGB tubes, bars or wall-washers uplighting the wall.
- **Second colour (optional):** a complementary edge stripe or pool. Teal with orange and teal with magenta are reliable pairs.
- **Rim (optional):** a small gridded source in the accent colour, 120-150° behind, at head height. Keep it off the cheek.
- **Practicals:** an RGB tube, neon or logo in frame to motivate the colour.

**Background.** Treat it as its own lighting zone, 2-4 m behind. Three treatments: (1) a hard circle of colour behind the head; (2) even uplit washes in one or two hues, with set dressing; (3) the whole room in one deep colour with only the key clean. A painted or coloured paper backdrop needs less light. Keep it 1-2 stops under the face for moody, level or brighter for pop. A subject's shadow is fine on a hard circle but looks sloppy on a wash.

**Camera.** Use 35-50 mm for set wides and 85 mm for the MCU or CU, which compresses the wall into a soft colour field. Shoot at f/2-f/2.8. Eyeline off-axis to the interviewer on the key side. B-cam: a wider 30-45° profile or two-shot. Centre the subject on the colour pool so it acts as a halo. Creator setups go on-axis to the lens.

**Colour.** Lock white balance to the key. Background gels fully saturated: teal (Lee 116, HSI hue about 180°), magenta (Lee 128, about 320°), deep blue (Lee 120, about 230°), and CTO plus red or amber for warm. The rim uses the second colour. Pull RGB saturation down 5-10% if a channel clips. Check skin on the vectorscope (a scope that shows colour) for colour spill.

**Small-kit version (3 lights).**
1. Key: a 120-150 cm octa or 90 cm parabolic on a COB at 5600 K, 35-45° on the short side, gridded.
2. Background: a COB with a fresnel or projection attachment and a gel, or an RGB panel, low behind the subject and aimed at the wall.
3. Optional: an RGB tube as a coloured rim or practical.

Use a white bounce under the lens and a black flag between the key and the wall. With two lights, skip the rim and use coloured paper.

**Common mistakes.**
- Key spill washing out the wall.
- Colour on the face (green or magenta skin).
- Too many colours, so it looks like a nightclub.
- RGB channels clipping into flat blobs.
- A rim creeping onto the cheek.
- The subject's shadow in the middle of a wash.
- Auto white balance pulling skin to the complementary colour.
- Background so dark the colour is lost. Aim for 1-2 stops under, not 4.
- A ring light or flat on-axis key in front of colour, which looks cheap.

**Prompt phrases.**
- studio interview portrait, soft neutral key light on the face, saturated teal background wash
- magenta gelled rim light on the hair, teal backdrop, clean natural skin tones
- seated subject in front of a cyc wall with a soft orange spotlight circle behind the head
- two-person podcast set, large octabox key, purple and teal RGB uplights on the back wall
- complementary colour background gradient, teal and orange gel light on seamless paper

## 7. Window-style soft side light in studio

Sub-board: https://www.cosmos.so/timmy111/window-style-soft-side-light-in-studio

**Look.** One big soft source to one side, reading as a window just out of frame. Light wraps the near cheek and falls off to a darker far cheek, with no hard nose shadow and one set of catchlights. The background sits 1-2 stops under, with small warm practicals. It feels calm and documentary, like "a study in the afternoon". Contrast is about 3:1 to 4:1 for moody versions and about 2:1 for corporate ones.

**Best for.**
- Brand-doc interviews with founders, CEOs and case studies
- Premium corporate and recruitment films
- Sports and club docs on dressed sets
- Two-camera sit-downs (A near the eyeline, B in profile)
- Moody long-form conversation
- Subjects in glasses (a big source off to the side keeps reflections out)

**Rig, light by light.**
- **Key (the window):** 6x6 to 8x8 ft, or 4x4 at minimum. Grid cloth with an LED or COB pushed through it, or a 5-7 ft parabolic feathered across. Place it 60-90° off the lens on the side the subject faces. The top edge sits at or above head height and the bottom near seat height, so it reads as a window, not a top light. Keep it 1-2 m from the face.
- **Negative fill:** a black 4x4 or solid close on the opposite side. It is what keeps the far cheek dark.
- **Fill (optional):** none for moody work. For corporate, a white reflector or V-flat near camera on the shadow side, lifting the far cheek by 1/2 to 1 stop. Never put fill on the key side.
- **Directional accent (optional):** a gridded 1x1 or 1x2 LED high on the window side for a sun-shaft feel, with haze if you want a beam.
- **Background practicals:** 2-3 dimmed warm lamps, or a painted backdrop lit by a small fresnel from the side.
- **Rim (optional):** a faint back-edge from the window side, 120-150° from camera.

**Background.** Keep the subject 2-4 m from the wall, and flag the key off it. Use a dressed room (shelves, plants, a couch, a curtained window) with deep green, navy or timber walls. A white room gives a brighter, lower-contrast version. A visible bright sheer curtain on the key side of frame "explains" the light.

**Camera.** Use 35-85 mm at f/2-f/2.8. A-cam just off the eyeline. The interviewer sits beside the lens on the key side, so the subject turns towards the window and the lens sees the short side. B-cam: 85-135 mm, 30-45° off on the opposite side, for a more lit three-quarter view. Leave headroom and lead room. Keep the mid-shot wide enough to show practicals. Camera at eye height or slightly below.

**Colour.** Key at 5000-5600 K, practicals at 2700-3200 K. Grade to neutral-warm skin with slightly cool shadows. For golden hour, set the key around 4300 K with a 1/4 CTO edge. Corporate versions stay neutral and low-saturation. Match LED sources for green/magenta.

**Small-kit version (3 lights).**
1. A 300-600 W COB through a 4x4 or 6x6 frame (or a 120-150 cm parabolic), 70-90° to one side, centre just above eye height, 1-1.5 m away, feathered. A black 4x4 or V-flat close on the opposite side.
2. A warm practical or small LED tube in the background, dimmed.
3. Optional: a gridded 60-100 W LED as a low back kick, or skimming the back wall.

Kill the overheads. Black out real windows that fight the key.

**Common mistakes.**
- Source too small or far away, so it looks like a light, not a window.
- Key within 30° of the lens, which flattens the face.
- No negative fill in a light room.
- Key spill on the back wall.
- Practicals clipping.
- The subject facing away from the key without meaning to (broad).
- Daylight key next to uncorrected cool-white LEDs.
- Real windows blowing out. Hold them 1-2 stops over the face.
- Glasses glare when the key is near the lens axis.
- B-cam on the key side, so both angles look the same.

**Prompt phrases.**
- seated documentary interview, soft window light from the side
- large diffused light source just out of frame camera left, gentle falloff across the face
- dark shadow side with negative fill, cinematic contrast
- deep green walls, bookshelves, floor lamp bokeh
- daylight key with warm tungsten practicals, gentle warm-cool contrast
- behind-the-scenes photo of an interview set with a large grid cloth frame acting as a window

## 8. Podcast and multi-person studio setups

Sub-board: https://www.cosmos.so/timmy111/podcast-and-multi-person-studio-setups

**Look.** Large soft sources overhead and in front, one per seat or per pair. Faces get gentle wraparound light. The table and mics sit in a pool, and the room falls to low-key. Depth comes from practicals, uplights and coloured washes, not hard rim. It feels warm, premium and "lean in and listen". The wide and the singles look like the same light, because each key is fixed and only the cameras move.

**Best for.**
- Long-form podcasts (Diary of a CEO, Modern Wisdom, Huberman style)
- Host-and-guest table interviews
- Three- or four-person panels and roundtables
- Founder and thought-leadership series
- Multi-hour, multi-camera shoots where lights cannot be re-set
- Branded corporate studio shows

**Rig, light by light.**
- **Key A (one per person):** a 1.2-1.5 m octa or Light Dome on a pipe or boom, 1-1.5 m above head height, 1.5-2.5 m away, aimed down 30-40°, 20-35° off that person's eyeline.
- **Key B:** a mirror of Key A, with the same modifier, power, height and Kelvin. Matched keys are what make the singles intercut. In a two-hander, each key often hangs over the other person's side and cross-keys.
- **Panel alternative:** two gridded 1x2 LED panels on a pipe over the table, one aimed at each person. Harder and more contrasty.
- **Table top (3-4 people):** a central softbox, chimera or rag over the table for base exposure, plus 2-4 strips or panels as individual keys and edges.
- **Shared rim (optional):** one big round source behind the table between two facing people. It needs low front fill, or the faces go to silhouette.
- **Fill:** usually none. The tabletop, the other key's spill and clothing fill it. If needed, use a frontal soft source 2-3 stops under near the A line. Black flags on the outside of each seat.
- **Flags:** grids or skirts on every key, a top flag against ceiling flare, and a cutter (a long thin flag) off the floor in front of the table.
- **Background practicals:** warm uplights, cyan or purple shelf strips, pendants or lamps. Dim them separately.

**Background.** The set sits 3-6 m behind and is lit on its own: a dark textured room (brick, slats, a gym, shelves) 1.5-3 stops under with pools of light, or a bright built backdrop (an LED volume or blue cyc) held at a deliberate exposure. Keep the floor around the table dark so the pool reads.

**Camera.** Three or four cameras. A wide master at 24-35 mm, square or three-quarter to the table, placed so the rig stays out of frame. Each person gets a 50-85 mm cross single, 20-40° off-axis, clean or past the other person's shoulder. Eyelines go to the other person, never the lens. Boom-arm mics in shot are part of the look. Use f/2-2.8 on the singles. Lock exposure and white balance across all cameras.

**Colour.** Every key at the same Kelvin, 4300-5600 K. The background carries colour: practicals and uplights at 2700-3200 K plus one accent (purple, teal or cyan). Keep the accent off the skin. Grade to warm mids with slightly cool or cyan shadows.

**Small-kit version (3 lights, 2 people).**
1-2. Two 90-120 cm gridded octas on booms, one per person, about 1 m above head height and in front, angled down 35°, 20-30° off the eyeline towards that person's single. Same model, power and Kelvin.
3. A small COB or tube with a gel or RGB raking the back wall from low, or two practical lamps.

Add a black flag outside each seat. For 3-4 people, swap the two octas for one big overhead softbox or 4x4 frame, add one gridded panel on the main speaker, and keep the background light.

**Common mistakes.**
- Keys that differ in Kelvin, power or height, so the singles look like different shows.
- The room lit flat and bright with no flags (see 1751031620).
- Keys too low (glare, flat face) or too high (no catchlight).
- The rig in the wide, or the octa flaring the lens.
- A back light behind a two-hander with no front exposure.
- Accent colour on the skin.
- Seats and cameras placed after lighting, so the key lands on the broad side.
- Mic arms blocking mouths or casting shadows from the overhead key.

**Prompt phrases.**
- two-person podcast interview at a wooden table
- large round soft key lights hung overhead, one per person
- background falls off into shadow with warm practical uplights on the wall
- broadcast microphones on boom arms in frame
- cinematic multi-camera video podcast set, off-axis eyeline, guest looking at the host
- low-key studio, pool of light on the table

## Cross-cutting interview rules

**Eyeline and short-side key.**
- Decide the seat, the interviewer and the cameras before you light.
- The interviewer sits right beside the A lens. The subject looks just off-camera.
- The key goes on the side the subject looks towards, so it lights the far cheek. The cheek nearest the lens stays in shadow. This is "short-side" lighting.
- Broad lighting (the key on the camera side of the face) flattens and widens the face. It is the most common mistake on every board.
- For direct-to-lens (Interrotron or teleprompter), keep the key 30-45° off and feather harder.

**Negative fill.**
- Shape the face by taking light away, not by adding fill.
- A black 4x4 floppy or V-flat 0.5-1.5 m off the shadow cheek.
- Black duvetyne on reflective floors and tables.
- In white or small rooms this is not optional.
- If you need fill, use a bounce near the lens 2-3 stops under the key. Never use a second softbox.

**Background separation and distance.**
- Subject 2-4 m from the background (3-5 m for void and high-key). The minimum is 1.5 m.
- Grid, skirt and flag the key so it never lights the wall.
- Light the background on its own, with its own units.
- Moody looks: background 1.5-4 stops under the face. High-key: 1-2 stops over.
- Keep a darker zone right behind the head and a brighter accent to one side.
- Practicals sit in the negative space beside the head, dimmed until they just read.

**Matching A and B cameras.**
- Keep B on the same side of the eyeline as A for short-side looks. Crossing it shows the broad side.
- Change both the focal length (for example 50 mm to 85-135 mm) and the angle (20-45°) so the cuts don't jump.
- Lock white balance, ISO, shutter and picture profile across all cameras. Match lens filtration.
- Check every camera for stands, flags and flare before rolling.

**Skin tones and natural texture.**
- Skin must keep its texture. Never plastic or waxy.
- Do not over-diffuse, smooth or beauty-filter. Soft light already flatters.
- Keep skin neutral to slightly warm. White balance on the key, not on a coloured scene.
- Never let coloured washes or bounce land on the face. Check the vectorscope.
- Deep skin tones: give more key and a little specular sheen. Don't lift the whole image. Expose properly, don't underexpose.
- Watch shiny foreheads under high-key and floor bounce. Use powder.
- Crush blacks in the grade, not in camera.

**Glasses reflections.**
- Move the key further round (60°+) and higher.
- Tilt the frames down slightly at the ears.
- Big window-style side light is the most forgiving setup.
- Check from every camera angle. Look for the interviewer and stands in the lenses.

**Multi-person consistency.**
- Identical keys per seat: same model, modifier, power, height, distance and Kelvin.
- Mark the floor and the light positions. Re-meter each new person in a series.
- For a testimonial series, keep the backdrop exposure fixed and match each face to it.
- Decide the seats and eyelines first, so every person gets a short-side key.

**Sound-friendly placement.**
- Keep the key and flags clear of the boom's path. The boom usually comes from above on the opposite side to the key, or from below, so its shadow does not cross the face or the wall.
- Watch boom shadows from overhead keys and from the rim.
- Avoid noisy fans. Use silent mode on COBs and switch off the hazer during takes.
- On podcasts, set the mic arms before final lighting so they don't block mouths or cast shadows.
- Keep stands and cables out of the sound person's path to the subject.

## Shoot-day checklist (one page)

**Before the day**
- [ ] Client, tone and platform agreed. Setup chosen from the chooser table.
- [ ] Wardrobe note sent: no pure white on high-key, no all-black on the void, no busy patterns. Mid or rich tones.
- [ ] Floor plan drawn: seat, interviewer, A/B/C cameras, key side, boom side.
- [ ] Kit list includes flags, floppies, duvetyne, grids, skirts, dimmers and gels.

**Room**
- [ ] Overheads off and windows blacked out (or deliberately used).
- [ ] Subject placed 2-4 m or more from the background. Floor marks down.
- [ ] Set dressed: practicals at several depths, nothing growing out of the head.

**Light (in this order)**
- [ ] Key: big, close, short side, 20-45° up, feathered, gridded.
- [ ] Negative fill on the shadow side. Floor black if shiny.
- [ ] Flags: key off the wall, off the floor, off the lens.
- [ ] Background lit separately to the target stops under or over the face.
- [ ] Practicals dimmed until they just read. No clipping.
- [ ] Rim or hair only if hair or clothes merge with the background.
- [ ] All sources matched in Kelvin and checked for green/magenta.

**Camera**
- [ ] A at 35-85 mm off the eyeline. B tighter, same side for short-side looks, 20-45° round.
- [ ] Wide or master locked off with the rig out of frame.
- [ ] White balance, ISO, shutter, profile and filtration matched across cameras.
- [ ] Exposure: face first (waveform), then background. Catchlights in both eyes.
- [ ] Skin checked on the vectorscope. No colour spill.

**Talent and sound**
- [ ] Stand-in check, then real talent. Re-check catchlights, glasses and shine (powder).
- [ ] Boom path clear, no boom shadows. Mic arms set. Fans silent, hazer off for takes.
- [ ] Multi-person: every seat metered the same.

**Before roll**
- [ ] Walk every camera for stands, flags, flare and reflections.
- [ ] Shoot a BTS still of the rig plus the monitor frame (for the board and for matching tomorrow).

### B. Things the cinematographer agent should know

1. **Ask four questions before proposing any interview light:** client type, tone, platform, and number of people. Then use the chooser table.
2. **Default to short-side key.** Place the interviewer and the cameras first. Flag any plan where the key ends up on the broad side.
3. **Negative fill before fill.** Never propose a second softbox as fill. Propose a black floppy, and a bounce only for brighter corporate looks.
4. **Always state background stops relative to the face** and subject-to-background distance. Treat the background as a separate lighting zone.
5. **Keep B-cam on the same side of the eyeline** for short-side looks. Always change both focal length and angle.
6. **Practicals are for motivation, not exposure.** Specify that they are dimmed and placed beside the head.
7. **Colour discipline:** the key is cooler than the practicals, accents stay off the skin, white balance locks to the key, one accent hue (two at most).
8. **Skin texture is non-negotiable.** Prompts should include "natural skin texture" and never ask for smoothing or a beauty glow. Add specific notes for deep skin tones (more key, specular sheen, correct exposure).
9. **Multi-person: matched keys.** Same model, power, height and Kelvin per seat. Lock settings across cameras.
10. **Sound check in every rig plan:** boom path, boom shadows, fan noise, hazer off during takes.
11. **Small-kit fallback:** every recommendation should also come in a 2-3 light version for small crews.
12. **Use the board references.** When describing a look, cite the element links in this guide as visual targets for image and video prompt generation.
